The aim is not to put booking online. It is to let the routine run itself, so the owner only steps in for the exceptions — without learning a complicated tool.

01 / The problem

How might every customer book easily — without adding work for the owner?

Everything lives in one person

WeChat + paper diary + memory.

One long service delays everyone after it

Times were written down; durations were not.

Plans change — and every change means messaging everyone

Illness or travel = messaging everyone again.

Customers have to ask before they can book

No WeChat, no easy way in.

✂

The owner

  • Runs the salon alone
  • WeChat + paper diary
  • Hours change with health and travel
  • Away for two weeks recently
  • Needs tools she can read at a glance

“Is anyone booked on Friday? Who do I need to tell?”

03 / Design principles

Booking rules that protect the owner

01

The routine runs itself

Owner handles exceptions only.

02

Duration decides availability

150-min gap: yes to a cut, no to a colour.

03

Only valid start times

No “17:45” — the day stays unfragmented.

04

Recommend, don’t decide

Suggested times reduce effort; any valid time stays open.

05

“Not open yet” ≠ “full”

Owner sets how far ahead to open; no same-day bookings.

06

Protect long gaps

Fill small gaps; keep big blocks free.

07

Never delete a booking

Closures move bookings to rescheduling.

08

Low learning cost

Starts from “today”, like her paper diary.

Deliberately not in V1

  • Online payment
  • Coupons & points
  • Multiple shops
  • Parallel services
  • No-show penalties
  • AI chat

08 / How it was built

The logic was fixed before any screen: pain points, V1 scope, three user flows, then technical rules for the AI tools. Visual polish came last.

  1. 01

    One booking core

    Availability, booking, rescheduling, closures and admin actions live in one shared TypeScript core used by both sides.

  2. 02

    Guardrails for AI

    A written spec, folder rules and a list of what the AI may and may not change kept generated code inside the design.

  3. 03

    Two front ends

    A customer flow and a shop diary, both phone-sized; the target is a WeChat mini program with a separate entry for non-WeChat customers.

  4. 04

    Public prototype

    The real booking rules are bundled to run in the browser, so anyone can try both sides without a server.

09 / Challenges, gaps & opportunities

What is hard

A low-confidence user

The owner is used to WeChat and a paper diary. Every extra step is a reason to go back to them, so the shop side had to shrink to four tabs.

Durations are uncertain

Hair length and colour change how long a service takes, so estimates can still overrun.

Two kinds of customer

WeChat suits Chinese customers but not everyone else. V1 serves WeChat; others are added by the owner until their own entry is ready.

Not done yet

  • Cloud storage and real WeChat sign-in
  • Reliable notifications after a closure
  • Interview and on-site test with the owner
  • Own entry for non-WeChat customers

Opportunities

Fewer messages

If customers can see free times themselves, most “are you open?” messages disappear.

A wider local audience

A non-WeChat entry opens the shop to local customers who would never add a private WeChat.

A pattern for other small shops

The same core could serve other one-person services with changing hours.

How success would be measured

  1. Owner tasks in four taps or fewer
  2. Share of bookings made without chatting
  3. Time spent rescheduling after a closure
  4. Owner still using it after a month

Proposed measures for a pilot, not results.

10 / Validation plan

Planned fieldwork · no findings claimed yet

The next phase connects the prototype to real people and settings. The following is a research plan, not a report of completed activities.

01

Observe a real booking

With permission, map how a request moves from a conversation to the diary, including changes and interruptions. Record steps without retaining private customer details.

02

Validate the operating rules

Confirm service lengths, opening windows, same-day policy, buffers and preferred language with the owner. Demo fixtures are not final business rules.

03

Test both sides of a change

Ask a customer to book and reschedule, then ask the owner to adjust a period and identify affected appointments. Record completion, errors, time and assistance.

11 / Progress & release plan

The public prototype runs the project’s real booking rules in the browser, with sample data stored only on the visitor’s device. The UI was written in Chinese for WeChat customers; an English layer is on by default. CloudBase persistence, real WeChat identity, reliable notifications and production deployment are unfinished. No real customer is charged or notified.

  1. Now · customer and shop prototype, public and runnable in the browser
  2. Next · owner interview, observation and two-sided task testing
  3. Then · refine service rules and run an agreed limited pilot
  4. Before release · persistent data, identity, notification delivery and recovery checks

Local implementation checks do not establish usability or business impact. Release and a real pilot require further validation and explicit approval.
