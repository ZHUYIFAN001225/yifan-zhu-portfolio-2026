Every image is real, and every image leads to a person you can book. A Creator’s portfolio is the user’s inspiration — the feed is the bridge between them.

01 / The problem

How might a picture someone loves lead straight to the person who can make it real?

“I love it, but I don’t know what it’s called.”

People have a picture, not a service name.

“Pinterest shows me looks, not who can do them.”

The maker may be abroad, unqualified or untraceable.

“I have to pick a salon before I see the work.”

Shops and stars come first; the person’s skill comes last.

“Instagram gives me likes, not bookings.”

Real work, every day — but few new customers.

Where this stands

Built from my product outlines, a whole-product interactive demo and a Glasgow logic and business review. Interviews and field testing come next; accounts, Creators, prices and distances in the demo are simulated.

User

Browse real work, save what they like, find who made it, compare and book.

Creator

Hairstylists, barbers, nail and makeup artists. Their portfolio is their profile, and it follows them if they change salons.

Pilot operator

Recruits the first local Creators, sets trust rules for original work and client consent.

  3. 03

    Users browse and match local work.

  4. 04

    New results return to the feed.

07 / Decisions & state logic

01

Location before ranking

An attractive match outside the chosen area cannot solve a local service need.

Prototype response
Apply the distance filter before tag-based ranking; offer an editable area when location access is unavailable.
What to validate next
Check whether participants understand the area limit and find relevant options.
02

Matching is not feasibility

Similar images alone cannot establish provider capability, price or time.

Prototype response
Keep work attribution and match labels explicit; connect selection to consultation and confirmation.
What to validate next
Check whether people distinguish inspiration, provider work and a confirmed service.
03

Keep both sides consistent

Availability and price can change between browsing and booking.

Prototype response
Booking conditions are rechecked on submit, and pending requests stay visible to both sides.
What to validate next
Test stale information, unavailable slots and declined requests before a real pilot.

08 / How it was built

I wrote the product specification first, then built the prototype with AI coding tools inside clear rules, so every screen follows the same logic.

  1. 01

    Specification

    One written spec covers both roles: feed, matching, booking, payment rules, cancellation and privacy.

  2. 02

    Prototype

    A mobile web app with about 40 routes across both roles. Data stays in the browser, on one demo clock in Europe/London time.

  3. 03

    Rules in code

    Search radius is a hard limit, a declined Creator is not shown again, pending requests expire, and the home address stays hidden until confirmed.

  4. 04

    Checks

    Automated checks run every main flow and edge case, and render every route in both roles.

51main-flow interaction checks
56edge-case checks
80route renders across both roles
Limits

Checks ran on a virtual DOM; the prototype has not yet been tested on real phones with real users.

09 / Challenges, gaps & opportunities

What is hard

Cold start is a supply problem

The feed only works if there are local Creators with real work nearby. A beautiful feed means nothing if no one in range can do the look.

Similar is not the same as possible

A picture cannot show hair condition, time or materials, so a consultation or quote step is still needed before booking.

Image rights and consent

“Real work” must be the Creator’s own, with client consent. The prototype still uses reference images collected for research; before any pilot they must be replaced with consented work from real Creators.

Not done yet

  • Real accounts, payments and notifications
  • Postcode geocoding and travel time
  • Real image matching (scores are demo values)
  • Testing with Glasgow Creators and users

Opportunities

Portfolio as acquisition

A Creator’s portfolio brings first-time customers, so a first-customer fee aligns platform income with real value.

Local demand insight

Requests show what people nearby are looking for, which Creators can act on.

Personal preview, if proven

An AI preview on your own photo could be a paid feature, once quality, speed and cost are tested.

How success would be measured

  1. Searches with similar work in range
  2. Match → message → booking conversion
  3. Creator reply time
  4. Repeat bookings and shared results

Proposed measures for a pilot, not results.

10 / Validation plan

Planned fieldwork · no findings claimed yet

The next phase connects the prototype to real people and settings. The following is a research plan, not a report of completed activities.

01

Explore the real journey

Ask consumers how they collect references, explain a look and decide whom to trust. Capture consented journey notes and anonymised examples.

02

Observe provider work

Discuss consultation, feasible requests, pricing and diary management with local providers. Record actual constraints before narrowing the pilot.

03

Test one complete task

Ask a participant to turn a chosen look into a suitable service request. Record completion, hesitation, misunderstood labels and recovery from no matches.

11 / Progress & release plan

Creator identities, reviews, distances, payments and messages are simulated. Matching uses tags and rules; style preview is a collage. Browser storage does not provide production authentication or cross-device sync. A separate, narrower V1 includes persistent APIs and an iOS source project, but is not a launched product.

  1. Now · connected whole-product mobile demo
  2. Next · interviews, observation and task-based testing
  3. Then · refine a limited local provider pilot
  4. Before release · real identity, provider onboarding, privacy, payments and notifications

Local implementation checks do not establish usability or business impact. Release and a real pilot require further validation and explicit approval.
